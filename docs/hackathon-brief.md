### HealthTech

#### Lost in Transit: Fragmented Medical Records and Repeated Care Across Providers

CONTEXT: Patients with long term conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disorders, or asthma often see multiple doctors across clinics, diagnostic labs, and pharmacies over the years. Each provider keeps its own records, usually as paper prescriptions, PDF reports, or photos shared over messaging apps, and patients are expected to carry this history themselves. When someone changes doctors, moves cities, or visits an emergency room, the history rarely goes with them.

PROBLEM: A patient's medical history exists, but it is scattered across providers and rarely available, or safely shareable, at the moment a clinician needs it. Several specific challenges follow from this:

- Scattered records: Prescriptions, lab reports, scans, and discharge summaries sit in different places and formats, with no single timeline of a patient's health.
- Unreliable recall: Patients struggle to remember past diagnoses, allergies, current medications, and doses. Clinicians in a short consultation often work from incomplete or wrong information.
- Repeated tests and conflicting treatment: Without prior results, tests are redone, and different doctors may prescribe overlapping or interacting medicines without knowing what the patient already takes.
- Insecure and difficult sharing: Handing records to a new doctor means forwarding files over chat or carrying folders, which is slow and leaves sensitive data exposed. There is no simple, secure way to share the right history with the right doctor, or to stop sharing it afterward.
- Broken continuity of care: When a patient changes doctors, moves cities, or arrives at an emergency room, the new clinician starts from scratch, with no view of earlier diagnoses, treatments, and test results.

IMPACT: Fragmented records lead to repeated diagnostic tests that cost patients money and time, delayed or incorrect treatment decisions, and avoidable medication errors, particularly dangerous in emergencies when a patient cannot explain their history. Chronic patients lose continuity of care each time they switch providers. At a larger scale, duplicated tests and preventable complications add to the strain on an already stretched healthcare system, while patients remain without real ownership of their own health information.

Data note: Teams may use simulated or publicly available medical records/data and do not need to integrate with real hospital systems.

### TravelTech

#### Real-Time Crisis Communication for Tourists During Extreme Weather Events

CONTEXT: When localized extreme weather events occur—such as sudden flash floods or severe cyclones—foreign tourists are highly vulnerable due to their lack of local geographic knowledge and language barriers.

PROBLEM: Local weather warnings, emergency broadcasts, and safe-zone declarations are fragmented and rarely translated or localized for tourists. Travelers struggle to verify which areas are actively flooded or unsafe and cannot easily access translated instructions from local authorities, forcing them to rely on chaotic, unverified social media rumors.

IMPACT: This communication gap leads to widespread panic, stranded travelers wandering into high-risk weather zones, and unnecessary strain on local rescue services during critical windows.

### CivicTech

#### Improving Transparency and Coordination in Public Works Projects

CONTEXT: Residents see roads dug up, drains under repair, and pipelines being laid in their neighbourhoods every day. These projects are carried out by different municipal and utility departments and contractors. Residents have little information about what is being done, who is responsible, or how long it will take, and the departments themselves often work without knowing what others have planned.

PROBLEM: Information about ongoing and planned public works is scattered across offices, notice boards, and informal sources, and rarely reaches the people it affects. Residents cannot tell the purpose, timeline, or responsible authority of a project, or why it is delayed. Poor coordination means the same road may be dug up several times in a few months by different departments. When work stalls or is done badly, there is no simple way to follow up, verify progress, or hold anyone accountable. Deadlines slip without explanation, and residents have no reliable way to plan around disruptions.

IMPACT: Prolonged and repeated construction causes traffic problems, safety hazards, business losses, and daily inconvenience. Wasted public money from poor coordination and duplicated work goes unnoticed. Without transparency, trust in local authorities falls, and residents feel unheard. Authorities also lose the chance to use citizen feedback to catch problems early.

## Submission Guidelines

### 1. Project Submission

Teams must submit their project repository (via GitHub) and a 2–5 minute demo video. The demo must be added to the project repository.
All submissions must occur before the deadline.

### 2. Code Requirements

The project must be developed during the duration of the hackathon.
Teams can use open-source libraries and APIs, but must disclose them.

### 3. Documentation

A README file must accompany the project explaining the problem statement, the solution, and the setup instructions.

### 4. Shortlisting and Round 2

All valid submissions will first be evaluated within their respective domains.
The top teams from each domain will be shortlisted for Round 2.
In Round 2, shortlisted teams will present and demonstrate their project to the judging panel.
The Round 2 presentation schedule and any additional presentation instructions will be communicated to the shortlisted teams.

## Evaluation Criteria

Each track will be judged based on the following parameters:

### 1. Innovation (20%)

- Originality and creativity of the idea
- Novel approach to solving the problem
- Distinctiveness compared to existing solutions

### 2. Technical Implementation (35%)

- Depth and complexity of the solution
- Code quality, structure, and robustness
- Handling of edge cases and overall system design

### 3. Feasibility (20%)

- Practicality of the solution in real-world scenarios
- Scalability and reliability
- Consideration of constraints such as performance and usability
- Scope for future development

### 4. Video Presentation (15%)

- Clear and structured demonstration of the project
- Explanation of the problem and solution
- Effective showcasing of key features and functionality

### 5. Deployment / Hosting (10%)

Deployment / Hosting is mandatory in an accessible form.

- Web Apps: Must be hosted and accessible via a public link
- Mobile Apps: Must be provided in an installable and testable format (APK / TestFlight / emulator-friendly build)
- Marks will be awarded based on the quality and completeness of deployment.
- Accessibility of the project (e.g., usable by judges without setup)
- Stability and proper functioning of the solution
- Overall effort in making the project usable in a real-world setting

## Additional Notes
